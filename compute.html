💻 Computational Chemistry From electrons to infrared spectra — CHEM 3376
🏠 Labs 💻 Computational Chemistry

The computational lab is split into short parts that build on each other. You start with the hydrogen atom, where the exact answer is known, and finish by running molecular dynamics on a machine-learned surface to explain why CO₂ absorbs infrared light. Every calculation runs in Python on the hub with PySCF; no prior programming experience is needed.

Click a button to open that part on the hub. Do the parts in order: later parts use results from earlier ones (if you missed one, a reference copy is used automatically). Every part has answer checks with feedback, an abstract and conclusions, and a Submit button.

Session 1 — Electrons and bonds

One 3-hour lab session: Part 1, then Parts 2 and 3.

Part 1 ⚛️ From the Hydrogen Atom to O₂ H · H₂⁺ · H₂ · O₂ with PySCF

Topics: hydrogen radial wavefunctions and their Gaussian (STO-nG) approximations; the variational principle and Hylleraas's helium calculation; H₂⁺ bonding and antibonding orbitals; electron correlation in H₂ (Hartree–Fock vs. full CI); O₂ with density functional theory.

You will be able to:

  • explain why every basis set is built from Gaussians, and what they miss at the nucleus;
  • use the variational principle to judge which approximate energy is better;
  • compute a bond length, bond energy and force constant from a potential-energy curve;
  • explain why O₂ is a triplet (magnetic) and why it does not absorb infrared light.

Part 2 🎯 Ab Initio Theory and Basis Sets: CO HF → MP2 → CCSD(T) — about 60 min

Topics: the ab initio hierarchy (Hartree–Fock, MP2, CCSD(T)); correlation energy; correlation-consistent basis sets cc-pVXZ; the CO potential-energy curve and its tiny dipole moment.

You will be able to:

  • describe what each level of theory adds and what it costs (N⁴, N⁵, N⁷);
  • show a result converging as the method and basis set improve;
  • connect the change of dipole with bond length, dμ/dr, to IR intensity.
✓ Answer checks · Abstract & Conclusions · Submit
Part 3 〰️ Quantum Vibrational Levels Schrödinger equation on a grid — about 50 min

Topics: the finite-difference method for the vibrational Schrödinger equation on the CCSD(T) curve of CO; anharmonicity; zero-point energy; overtones; isotope shifts.

You will be able to:

  • turn a Hamiltonian into a matrix and find its energy levels as eigenvalues;
  • explain why the observed fundamental (2143 cm⁻¹) lies below the harmonic value;
  • predict how levels shift for 13CO.

Session 2 — Molecules in motion: CO₂ and the greenhouse effect

One 3-hour lab session: Parts 4, 5 and 6.

Part 4 🌍 CO₂ Normal Modes and IR Hessian · normal modes — about 60 min

Topics: equilibrium geometry; the mass-weighted Hessian and normal modes (3N − 5 for a linear molecule); IR intensities from the change of dipole; symmetry selection rules.

You will be able to:

  • find vibrational frequencies by diagonalising a mass-weighted Hessian;
  • decide from symmetry which modes are IR-active;
  • explain why CO₂ absorbs infrared light when O₂ does not (the bend).
✓ Answer checks · Abstract & Conclusions · Submit
Part 5 🤖 A Machine-Learned Surface Gaussian-process regression — about 60 min

Topics: learning a potential-energy surface and dipole model for CO₂ from a few hundred DFT calculations; symmetry-adapted coordinates; training and held-out test sets; prediction uncertainty.

You will be able to:

  • build the physics (translation, rotation, identical atoms) into a model's inputs;
  • judge a fit on data it has never seen, not on its training error;
  • check a learned surface against physical quantities such as harmonic frequencies.
✓ Answer checks · Abstract & Conclusions · Submit
Part 6 🎞️ Molecular Dynamics and the IR Spectrum Velocity Verlet · Fourier transform — about 60 min

Topics: classical molecular dynamics on the learned surface; the velocity Verlet integrator and energy conservation; the IR spectrum as the Fourier transform of the dipole μ(t); rotational P and R branches.

You will be able to:

  • write an integrator for Newton's equations and test it by energy conservation;
  • compute a spectrum from a trajectory and compare it with the harmonic sticks of Part 4;
  • explain the shape of a gas-phase IR band.
✓ Answer checks · Abstract & Conclusions · Submit

Project week — Your own molecule

Independent work, ending in an abstract and one or two figures.

Part 7 🔬 Project: Electrons to an IR Spectrum N₂O, OCS, HCN, CS₂, H₂O or SO₂

Topics: the full CO₂ workflow for a molecule you choose: geometry, normal modes and IR intensities, a learned surface and dipole, MD at two temperatures, and the spectrum compared with experiment.

You will be able to:

  • plan and carry out a complete computational study on your own;
  • compare harmonic, dynamical and experimental frequencies and explain the differences;
  • report the result as a short scientific abstract with figures.
